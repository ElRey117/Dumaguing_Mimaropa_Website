Our History & Culture

Stories, traditions, and heritage across five provinces.

The islands of MIMAROPA were home to Indigenous communities long before Spanish colonization. Groups such as the Mangyan of Mindoro and the Tagbanua and Palaw'an of Palawan have kept their traditions, languages and crafts alive.

Spanish colonial rule left stone churches, towns and religious customs, which are still visible in places like Boac, Marinduque. Island life, farming and fishing shaped each province in its own way.

MIMAROPA was created as a region in 2002, and its name combines Mindoro, Marinduque, Romblon and Palawan. Preserving local traditions helps keep the region's identity strong for future generations.

Occidental Mindoro

Mountains, beaches and the coral waters of Apo Reef.

  • Mangyan heritage
  • Traditional weaving
  • Coastal and farming communities

Oriental Mindoro

Island escapes, rich traditions and the poetry of the Mangyan.

  • Mangyan weaving and ambahan poetry
  • Bahaghari Festival

Marinduque

Faith, culture and the famous masks of Holy Week.

  • Moriones Festival (Holy Week)
  • Heritage churches
  • Uraro cookies

Romblon

Islands, marble and local treasures made by hand.

  • Romblon marble carving
  • Biniray Festival
  • Local handicrafts

Palawan

World-renowned beauty with living Indigenous cultures.

  • Tagbanua and Palaw'an cultural heritage
  • Baragatan sa Palawan
  • Basketry and weaving
  • Cashew products and tamilok
